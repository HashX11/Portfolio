UAE Digital Twin

A national-scale digital twin of the United Arab Emirates, running in real time in Unreal Engine. I build the cities themselves — modeled in 3ds Max, textured in Substance, delivered through Datasmith, geolocated with GIS data.

Unreal Engine3ds MaxDatasmithSubstanceGISArabic / English UI

My role: the 3D city pipeline end-to-end — footprint extraction, low-poly modeling, UV mapping, texturing, geolocation, naming and tagging, collision, optimization, and delivery into engine, at LOD 100–200 across the seven Emirates.

3.1

Bungalow Resort — Rize, Türkiye

HospitalityMountain siteConcept → client

A mountain resort on a working tea plantation above Rize, and the whole scheme is drawn from the tea service itself. The main lodge is a teapot; every guest unit is a Turkish teacup cut in half lengthwise and laid on its side — not a barrel — its curved shell stepped along the terraced slope so each roof reads as another contour of the hillside. Developed from drone site survey through concept alternatives and eight client submissions to full presentation visuals.

4.3

Al Tall Regeneration — UNDP

A UNDP-backed regeneration of the Ottoman clock-tower square at the heart of Tripoli. Mohamad Hashem authored the full 3D reconstruction and every visualisation, then ran the science underneath: solar comfort, wind, shadow and pedestrian simulations that shaped where people would actually sit, walk and stay.

3ds MaxCoronaLadybug / HoneybeePhotogrammetry

The model was rebuilt from drone photogrammetry of the real district; proposals were tested against annual comfort and wind data before a single render was framed. All modeling and all renders on this page are Mohamad Hashem's.

Client
UNDP · Municipality of Tripoli
Practice
LDRS Architects
Role
Full 3D · visualisation · analysis
Site
Al Tall square · Tripoli, Lebanon
·

Walking it

The scheme was delivered to UNDP as a full walkthrough film — the square at eye level, the way a Tuesday afternoon would actually feel. Two passes from it: down the relaid tram street, and up the new water garden to the foot of the clock tower.

·

The evidence layer

Design decisions were argued with data — annual comfort zones, solstice shadows, wind velocity, pedestrian stay-places, commercial heat — down to algorithmically placed café locations scored against the comfort map. Some layers were built the slow way — walking the site, counting, measuring, then turning the field notes into diagrams; others were simulated computationally in Grasshopper with Ladybug and Honeybee.
